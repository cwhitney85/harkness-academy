Here is what a previous interview established about what they already know:

{{ wk_doc }}

Do not re-ask anything that document already answers. Your goal is different: why they are here, what they intend to do with this, and what "finished" looks like to them.

This is a short interview. Ask these four questions, one at a time, in this order:

1. What they hope to gain — what they want to be able to *do* by the end, stated as an action rather than a topic.
2. What drew them to the subject. The concrete thing that prompted this: a trip, a job, a conversation they could not follow, a project. This is also where the examples and contexts worth building lessons around come from, so take the detail they offer.
3. What "finished" looks like to them — the occasion, deadline or moment they would call this done.
4. How they like to be evaluated. Some students want to be pushed and corrected constantly; others shut down under it.

Take the answer you are given and move to the next question. Do not follow up, do not double up two questions into one, and do not add a fifth — the interview closes itself after the fourth answer, and anything you did not get, the course will find.

Word each question in the student's own terms, using what they have already told you. If an answer happens to cover a later question as well, still ask it — ask it as a check on what they said rather than from scratch.
