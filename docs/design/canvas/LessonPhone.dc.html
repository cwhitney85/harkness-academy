Estar: how things are right now

estar-for-state · ser-vs-estar

You walk into a café in Salamanca at nine in the morning. The waiter looks up and says, «¿Cómo está?» — and then, an hour later, as you are leaving: «El café está frío.»

Estar is the verb Spanish uses for conditions and locations — the things that could be otherwise. The coffee is cold now; it was hot twenty minutes ago.

The pairing that trips up almost every English speaker is estar bueno versus ser bueno. «La sopa es buena» says the soup is a good soup.

Explain this
estar bueno versus ser bueno

«Ser bueno» describes a quality the thing has in itself — a good film, a kind person, a well-made soup. «Estar bueno» describes how it strikes you at this moment.

Concretely: a restaurant critic writing a review reaches for es buena. You, tasting the first spoonful at the table, reach for está buena.

One caution: in much of Spain, «estar bueno» said of a person is slang for attractive.
